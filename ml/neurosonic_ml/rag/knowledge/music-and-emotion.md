# Music and Emotion Research

Music reliably influences affect and physiological arousal, and listeners can perceive emotion in music with considerable agreement. Perceived emotion (what the music expresses) and induced emotion (what the listener feels) are related but not identical.

## Musical features and perceived emotion
Research on music emotion perception reports consistent associations:
- Tempo: faster tempi are associated with higher perceived arousal; slower tempi with calm or sadness.
- Mode: major mode is more often perceived as happy or positive, minor mode as sad or tender, though this is culturally shaped.
- Loudness and dynamics: louder, more dynamic music is perceived as more intense.
- Timbre and brightness: brighter spectra are associated with higher arousal and energy.
- Rhythmic regularity and density: dense, accentuated rhythm raises perceived energy; sparse rhythm and sustained pads feel calmer.
- Harmonic complexity and dissonance: greater dissonance is associated with tension.

## Physiological effects
Listening to slow, soft music has been associated with reductions in heart rate and self-reported stress in several studies, while fast, loud music tends to increase arousal. Effects are modest, vary between individuals and depend on personal preference and familiarity.

## Implications for NeuroSonic
NeuroSonic maps a mood point (valence, arousal) onto music parameters following these tendencies: arousal drives tempo, rhythmic density and dynamics; valence drives mode and brightness. These are design heuristics grounded in perception research, not guarantees that a given piece will change how a particular person feels.
