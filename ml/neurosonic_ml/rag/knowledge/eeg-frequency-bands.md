# EEG Frequency Bands

Electroencephalography (EEG) records voltage fluctuations at the scalp, produced mainly by synchronized postsynaptic activity of large populations of cortical neurons. The signal is conventionally described by frequency bands. Exact boundaries differ between papers; NeuroSonic uses delta 1–4 Hz, theta 4–8 Hz, alpha 8–13 Hz, beta 13–30 Hz and gamma 30–45 Hz.

## Delta (1–4 Hz)
Delta is the dominant rhythm in deep, slow-wave sleep. In awake adults, small amounts are normal; large delta amplitude while awake can reflect drowsiness, or contamination by eye and movement artifacts at frontal sites.

## Theta (4–8 Hz)
Theta increases with drowsiness and light sleep. Frontal-midline theta has also been reported to increase during sustained mental effort and working-memory load, so theta is ambiguous: it can point toward either low arousal or internally directed attention, depending on context.

## Alpha (8–13 Hz)
Alpha is the classic "idling" rhythm, strongest over posterior regions with eyes closed and relaxed wakefulness. Alpha power generally decreases (alpha desynchronization) when a person opens their eyes, attends to visual input, or engages in demanding tasks. Higher alpha is therefore often interpreted as relaxed, low-arousal wakefulness, although alpha also plays an active role in inhibiting task-irrelevant regions.

## Beta (13–30 Hz)
Beta is associated with alert, active thinking and motor preparation. Increased beta is commonly linked to arousal, concentration or anxiety/tension, and cannot by itself distinguish between them. Scalp muscle activity (EMG) also contaminates the high-beta range.

## Gamma (30–45 Hz and above)
Gamma has been linked to local cortical processing, attention and perceptual binding, but at the scalp it is easily contaminated by muscle activity and line noise, so consumer-grade gamma estimates are unreliable.

## Important caveat
Band power shows tendencies at a group level. The same pattern can mean different things in different people, tasks and recording setups, so band power is a weak, indirect signal about emotion.
